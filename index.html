Study Notes Handler

My notes app for college. Every semester, subject and topic gets its own folder, and every file is one search away.

  • Published to Google Play (Early Access) on my own, through Google's 12-tester closed test and every compliance check, with no rejected submissions.
  • Works offline on an Isar database, with tags, search across every note, and progress reports drawn with fl_chart.
  • A built-in AI tutor on the Gemini API that keeps saved chats, plus a focus-music player that floats over your notes.

    ProofPitch

    An internship app for Indian college students. It gets you in front of a real person with proof of your work, not another Easy Apply.

    • Reads your resume PDF on the phone and fills in your whole profile.
    • Pulls live listings from Unstop, company careers pages (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters) and Telegram job channels, and blocks the ones you can't qualify for.
    • Finds who's hiring, drafts the email from the job post, attaches a resume tailored to the role, and reminds you to follow up. Cold-pitch mode finds small studios and startups to pitch directly.
    • No backend: your data stays on your phone. Covered by 10 test suites.

    PhoneLab

    A smartphone torture simulator. Spec a phone, then drop it, drown it, shoot it or run DOOM on it until it overheats.

    • Spec any device down to its glass, frame, case and IP rating, or load one of 13 presets, from the iPhone 16 Pro Max to a 1989 Game Boy.
    • Drop it in a 3D rigid-body arena where tumbling physics decides which face, edge or corner lands: stairs, a pool, a frozen lake, a volcano, the Moon.
    • Run DOOM, Minecraft or an LLM on it. Apps slow down as RAM swaps and the chip throttles, and with thermal protection off the battery swells and catches fire.
    • Plain JavaScript with no build step or libraries, including its own canvas 3D renderer. The damage model is pure functions, checked by a 53-assertion test script.

    WritersBlock

    A floating notes drawer for Android. Pull it out over any app to write, or scan part of the screen straight into a note.

    • A floating scroll handle sits over every app and unrolls a parchment drawer. It's a Compose UI hosted inside a foreground service, sharing one editor with the app itself.
    • Scan the screen: crop any region and ML Kit OCR puts both the picture and its text into the note. The text stays an ordinary paragraph, so OCR mistakes are fixed in place.
    • Notes are plain text files with a folder for their images, autosaved so nothing is lost, and shareable or exportable as PDF or Word.
    • A castle-at-night theme where every shape and icon is drawn on Canvas, with no image assets or icon library. Working on the Android emulator; not yet tested on a physical phone.

    Project Bonds

    A visual novel where every choice changes how each character feels about you, and some choices end the story.

    • One reusable C# event controller runs the branching dialogue, the choices and the UI state, across about 50 scripts.
    • Each character has a bond meter, and some replies only unlock with enough SPEECH, ANGER or SMARTS. Choices have consequences with their own game-over endings, and some scenes are one-offs: a convince minigame, riddles, a chase.
    • Assets load and unload on a fixed lifecycle, so scene transitions don't drop frames or leak memory.

    Project Rogue

    A turn-based roguelike. Survive wave after wave, and pick up new moves between fights.

    • A battle state machine (start, player turn, enemy turn, choosing, lost) runs every fight.
    • Moves are ScriptableObjects typed as attack, heal, buff or debuff, so a new move is data, not new code.
    • All damage goes through one deterministic formula with stacked buffs and debuffs. Fight one on the right; the log shows the math.
    • Enemies get stronger every wave, and between waves you visit a facility to swap moves.

    How Far Will It Sink?

    A browser game. Two objects are dropped over the Mariana Trench: pick the one that sinks deeper in 30 minutes. One wrong guess and it's game over.

    • 545 objects, from a potato to the Titanic, plus all 151 original Pokémon built from their official Pokédex height and weight.
    • Every depth comes from real physics: an object's real mass and size decide whether it floats, and its drag decides how fast it falls. Each result shows the weight and density used.
    • A dive through all five ocean zones with depth-appropriate sea life, down to the Challenger Deep at 10,935 m.
    • It gets hard fast: by a score of 10 the two depths are within a few percent. A test script checks the formula against measured sinking speeds and every object's mass against its size.
